Questions people ask before they wait on a septic truck

Do I need the tank pumped or only inspected?

Those are different jobs. A pump-out empties the tank. An inspection that never opens the lid can miss a full tank. Ask which one you are booking and whether both happen the same day.

Why does opening the lid matter?

Sludge depth and baffles are inside the tank. A yard walk cannot see them. Ask if the quoted visit includes uncovering and opening the lid.

What if they cannot find the tank?

Ask the price of a locate-only visit before anyone digs. Keep a prior drawing or county record if you have one. Do not pay a full pump price for a short probe with no pump-out unless that was the written deal.

Does septic pumping in Mecklenburg County need a permit?

Routine pumping is usually maintenance. New tanks, replacements, and many repairs need a county on-site wastewater permit. Confirm with Mecklenburg County Public Health or the environmental health office in Union, Cabarrus, Gaston, Iredell, or Lincoln if that is where you live.

How often should a Charlotte home pump the tank?

A common interval is three to five years. High water use shortens it. Ask the technician to measure sludge instead of selling a calendar date.

What should be in writing after the visit?

Ask for tank size if known, sludge and scum notes, baffle condition, whether the field was checked, and a clear yes or no on whether the tank was pumped.

Who licenses septic work in North Carolina?

County environmental health offices issue on-site wastewater permits. Ask which permit applies before anyone replaces a tank or field. Charlotte phone numbers are listed separately so you can ask the shop the same question.
